# Fleshmass Collision Design: V4

## Status And Purpose

This is the superseded V4 design for a future Zombieland interaction with
RimWorld Anomaly's fleshmass heart. It was replaced by
[`FLESHBEAST-v5.md`](FLESHBEAST-v5.md) after the rage-control-flow, corner-touch,
throughput, and smash-setting review. V4 remains historical design evidence.
V3 and the earlier documents are retained for the same purpose:

- [`FLESHBEAST-v1.md`](FLESHBEAST-v1.md) preserves the original displacement
  fantasy and alternatives.
- [`FLESHBEAST-v2.md`](FLESHBEAST-v2.md) records the first safety review.
- [`FLESHBEAST-v3.md`](FLESHBEAST-v3.md) records the queue-and-reservation design
  that V4 deliberately removes.

V4 keeps the original player-facing idea but changes its implementation model.
Zombies do not maintain a separate relocation system. A horde that becomes
enraged and is locally blocked by outer active fleshmass may attack the obstructing
cell through normal zombie building-attack behavior. When a cell dies, vanilla
Anomaly performs its normal local fleshmass collapse. The heart later grows and
responds through its own vanilla systems.

The result is not mathematically equal displacement. Some collisions help the
player, some make the battlefield worse, and many do nothing. That unevenness is
intentional. The balancing cost is the enraged horde, the loss of precise player
control, and the vanilla fleshbeast response generated by accumulated destroyed
mass.

This document is design and implementation guidance. It does not claim that the
feature has been implemented or live-tested.

## Executive Decision

Use a **rare rage-obstruction interaction with vanilla damage, vanilla cascade,
vanilla regrowth, and vanilla fleshbeast response**.

Do not implement:

- a relocation queue;
- pending expansion credits;
- replacement reservations;
- hourly custom pulses;
- recent-wound memory;
- a new fleshmass target index;
- heart scent or map-wide attraction;
- a new zombie attack mode;
- a new player setting;
- a dedicated fleshmass job;
- a one-cell custom removal operation; or
- a Zombieland growth algorithm.

The only new gameplay rules are:

1. A raging zombie that is unable to continue normal rage movement may treat
   an adjacent, safe outer `Fleshmass_Active` cell as a biological obstruction.
2. The zombie attacks that cell using its ordinary `AttackStatic` flow and
   ordinary melee damage.
3. Fleshmass killed by a Zombieland-faction instigator advances the same existing
   response counter as vanilla player-caused fleshmass destruction.
4. Zombies may not deliberately attack within a conservative protected core
   around any spawned fleshmass heart.

Everything else remains vanilla or existing Zombieland behavior.

## What RimWorld 1.6 Actually Does

The final design depends on the current installed RimWorld 1.6 behavior. These
facts must be re-audited before implementation if the game assembly changes.

### Damage And Cascade

`Fleshmass_Active` inherits ordinary fleshmass hit points. Merely damaging a cell
does not collapse the surrounding mass. The collapse begins when the cell is
killed.

`CompCascadeOnDestroyed` then:

- chooses a random cascade count from **4 through 8**;
- traverses cardinally adjacent vanilla cascade-eligible fleshmass;
- kills at most that many additional cells;
- passes the original instigator into each additional kill; and
- marks the additional kills with `PreventCascade`, so those cells do not each
  start another cascade.

One killed root cell therefore removes between **1 and 9 total cells**, normally
between **5 and 9** when enough connected mass exists. A field edge, short
tendril, map boundary, or earlier damage can end the cascade before the random
maximum is reached.

The cascade may include both `Fleshmass` and `Fleshmass_Active`, exactly as
vanilla defines. V4 does not replace or filter the cascade after it starts.

### Heart Notification And Fleshbeast Response

Every destroyed sourced fleshmass cell notifies its source grower that mass was
destroyed, causing vanilla connectivity and tendril state to be reconsidered on
later growth.

Vanilla separately tracks player-caused destruction. A heart begins with a
random threshold of **125 through 200 destroyed cells**. Each qualifying
destroyed cell decrements that counter. At zero, vanilla:

- chooses fleshbeasts from the current threat level;
- births them from nearby active fleshmass;
- creates a fleshbeast assault lord;
- plays the vanilla sound, effects, camera shake, and threat letter; and
- resets the threshold to another random value from 125 through 200.

The response is not emitted for every root cell or every cascade. It is a
periodic consequence of accumulated destruction.

### Why A Small Zombieland Classification Patch Is Required

Vanilla advances that response counter only when the damage instigator has no
faction or belongs to the player. Normal Zombieland zombies are assigned the
dedicated Zombieland zombie faction, so their kills currently:

- perform the vanilla cascade; but
- do **not** advance the vanilla fleshbeast-response counter.

Without correction, rare zombie attacks would mostly be free cleanup for the
player. V4 therefore extends only the classification boundary: sourced
fleshmass killed by an instigator from the Zombieland zombie faction counts once
toward the same vanilla response counter.

This must happen once for every actual root or cascade cell that vanilla kills.
It must not award a fixed amount per attack, predict the random cascade size, or
invoke `DoFleshbeastResponse` directly.

### Vanilla Growth Is The Resurgence

The heart already owns:

- rapid growth-point spending;
- tendril and core growth;
- thickening;
- nerve-bundle and spitter growth;
- independent fleshbeast spawning; and
- periodic growth cycles and mini-growth.

V4 does not mint replacement points when zombies destroy flesh. The heart may
regrow the lost area quickly, much later, or elsewhere according to its existing
state. It may also remain locally diminished until another vanilla growth cycle.

That lack of one-for-one conservation is intentional. Strict conservation was
the source of most V2 and V3 complexity. The encounter already has a biological
recovery system and a defender-response system; V4 uses them.

## Review Of V1 Through V3

### V1: Strong Fantasy, Unsafe Mechanism

V1 correctly identified the entertaining result: zombies damage one part of the
field and living flesh later surges somewhere else. It also correctly resisted
turning zombies into a direct heart-kill solution.

Its weak points were implementation assumptions:

- ordinary destruction was treated as if it removed one cell;
- vanilla growth points were considered as relocation currency;
- heart exposure was not safe after analysis;
- wound memory and directional weighting added long-lived state;
- defender spending and special-zombie modifiers expanded the feature before
  its core loop existed; and
- unrestricted zombie participation risked constant map-wide flesh mining.

V4 preserves V1's emotional result but obtains it from intermittent vanilla
collapse and vanilla regrowth.

### V2: Correct Safety Discoveries, Incorrect Loss Policy

V2 correctly established:

- exact source-heart ownership matters;
- normal destruction cascades;
- the final heart approach must remain protected;
- vanilla growth points cannot represent hourly one-for-one relocation;
- fleshmass must not enter the pawn target index; and
- deferred work must be bounded.

Its queue policy expired, evicted, or consumed unplaceable relocation units.
That made blocked placement a permanent deletion sink. The system could say a
cell had been relocated even though no replacement existed.

### V3: Strong Transactional Reasoning, Too Much System

V3 fixed V2's deletion sink by reserving capacity before removal and retaining
blocked units. It also fixed repeated one-tick chewing by defining a timed
action.

The cost was a second fleshmass state machine containing:

- per-heart map state;
- active reservations;
- committed units;
- exact origins;
- queue capacity;
- enqueue timestamps;
- hourly scheduling;
- bounded retry searches;
- origin snapshots;
- save/load cancellation; and
- extensive recovery rules.

Even then, up to 48 cells could remain absent while waiting for legal
destinations. The default `OnlyHumans` mode also disabled the mechanic, while
`Everything` did not semantically fit a stationary biological building.

V4 removes the transaction rather than making it more elaborate.

## Alternatives Considered For V4

### Atomic One-For-One Relocation

This would find a destination, place a new cell, and retract the old cell in one
atomic action.

Advantages:

- no queue or permanent debt;
- exact mass conservation;
- easy blocked-space behavior; and
- clear source ownership.

Reasons not chosen:

- still requires a custom placement algorithm;
- suppresses the characteristic vanilla collapse;
- bypasses vanilla destruction-driven fleshbeast response unless recreated;
- needs graph-safe one-cell removal to avoid detached tendrils;
- replaces Anomaly's biological rhythm with a Zombieland transaction; and
- makes every completed gnaw mechanically tidy, which is less chaotic than the
  intended collision.

Atomic relocation remains preferable to V3 if strict one-for-one displacement
ever becomes mandatory, but strict conservation is not mandatory for V4.

### Random Eating Chance For Every Adjacent Zombie

This would treat fleshmass like a corpse and periodically let any adjacent
eating-capable zombie gnaw it.

Advantages:

- local and thematically understandable;
- easy to make probabilistic; and
- naturally scales with zombie count.

Reasons not chosen:

- adds another recurring scan and random timer;
- needs a new probability or mean-time tuning value;
- large idle crowds still create many independent attempts;
- competes with the existing downed-pawn and corpse eating priorities;
- makes calm single zombies environmental attackers; and
- does not use the existing horde-agitation system that already represents
  dangerous group pressure.

### A Fixed Percentage Of Flesh-Curious Zombies

This would derive a stable hidden trait from zombie identity and let only that
subset attack flesh.

Advantages:

- no serialized state if derived deterministically;
- simple population scaling; and
- easy percentage tuning.

Reasons not chosen:

- creates an invisible new zombie behavior class;
- does not explain why a calm wanderer suddenly attacks terrain;
- remains active continuously once a qualifying zombie reaches flesh; and
- introduces a new balance dial instead of reusing rage level.

### Raging Zombies Attack Blocking Flesh: Chosen

Rage already means:

- a dense horde has become dangerous;
- affected zombies stop behaving like calm wanderers;
- they move purposefully toward the colony;
- they smash obstructions when movement fails;
- the player can disable the behavior; and
- the rage-level setting controls how readily groups trigger it.

Fleshmass therefore becomes one more biological obstruction that a genuinely
raging zombie may attack when it cannot continue. This is the smallest rule
that produces rare, horde-scaled, player-readable pressure without adding a new
mode.

## Final V4 Player-Facing Behavior

A calm zombie does not care about active fleshmass. It does not smell it, seek
it, index it, eat it, or attack it merely because it is adjacent.

When a dense zombie group triggers the existing rage behavior, individual
raging zombies try to follow their existing rage movement toward the colony. If
a raging zombie cannot make a valid move and a safe outer active-fleshmass cell
is one of the local biological obstructions, that zombie may attack the cell.

The zombie uses normal melee damage. It may take several attacks, several
zombies, or a substantial part of the rage duration to kill the cell. There is
no special instant-chew damage.

When the cell dies, vanilla collapses up to four through eight additional
connected fleshmass cells. The opening may let the horde continue, expose useful
ground, destroy part of a tendril, or merely change the edge.

Every destroyed sourced cell advances the existing vanilla fleshbeast-response
counter. Eventually fleshbeasts burst from nearby active mass using the
vanilla threat-scaled response. Existing Zombieland Anomaly hostility settings
then decide whether zombies and those fleshbeasts attack or ignore one another.

The heart continues its normal growth. It may reclaim ground, grow elsewhere,
produce organs, or create independent defenders. V4 does not direct that
response.

The player may gain an opening. The price may be an enraged horde moving through
it, a later fleshbeast eruption, or growth in a worse location. There is no
promise that each event is neutral or fair.

## Settings Contract

V4 introduces no setting.

| Existing setting | V4 effect |
| --- | --- |
| `ragingZombies == false` | No deliberate V4 fleshmass attacks occur. Incidental damage from existing weapons, explosions, fire, or other systems remains possible. |
| `ragingZombies == true` | A zombie can become eligible only while its existing `raging` state is active. |
| `zombieRageLevel` | Remains the player-facing frequency and severity dial through its existing horde-size threshold and rage duration. V4 adds no second probability. |
| `smashMode == Nothing` | Absolute opt-out: V4 does not deliberately attack fleshmass. |
| `smashMode == DoorsOnly` | Raging zombies may attack safe blocking active fleshmass as a narrow biological-obstruction exception. Calm zombies still attack no fleshmass. |
| `smashMode == AnyBuilding` | The same V4 rage-only rule applies. Generic building-smash scanning must not make fleshmass a continuous calm-zombie target. |
| `smashOnlyWhenAgitated` | V4 is stricter than this setting because it always requires actual rage. Changing the checkbox does not make calm zombies attack flesh. |
| `attackMode` | No effect. Fleshmass is not a creature target and is never added to the normal attack index. |
| `zombiesEatCorpses` / `zombiesEatDowned` | No effect. V4 is obstruction-smashing, not feeding. |
| Anomaly targeting overrides | No effect on the fleshmass attack itself. They remain authoritative for pawn-versus-pawn hostility after fleshbeasts appear. |

The `DoorsOnly` help text will need a small player-facing clarification when V4
ships: raging zombies may also tear at blocking living fleshmass. No new control
or settings section is justified.

## Exact Decision Order

V4 participates only in the existing `JobDriver_Stumble` flow. It does not add a
new global tick loop.

For each relevant zombie decision:

1. Preserve all existing death, paralysis, downing, wall-push, roping, and other
   hard-state checks.
2. Preserve ordinary adjacent pawn attack selection. A valid creature target
   always wins over V4.
3. Preserve valid current movement. V4 cannot interrupt a zombie that can
   continue its existing rage route.
4. Preserve the existing downed-pawn and corpse-eating decision where it already
   precedes the rage-movement fallback.
5. Enter the existing rage-movement obstruction path only if the zombie is
   currently raging.
6. Let existing door, tank, suicide-bomber, and ordinary smash rules retain
   their current priorities.
7. If no higher-priority action succeeds and rage movement is blocked, evaluate
   adjacent active fleshmass through the V4 eligibility rules.
8. If one eligible cell exists, start the ordinary one-attack `AttackStatic`
   job against that cell.
9. If none exists, continue existing diversion, crowd movement, ordinary smash,
   or rage-ending behavior.

This decision is deterministic once all gates are satisfied: an eligible cell
starts one ordinary attack. V4 adds no separate attack-chance roll. Its
occasional nature comes from existing rage occurrence and duration, local
pathing, obstruction, adjacency, higher-priority actions, ordinary attack
timing, and survival. Randomized cardinal order only breaks a tie between
several eligible cells.

V4 must not be checked by the normal high-frequency pawn attack scan and must
not be inserted into `ZombieAttackTargetIndex`.

## Attacker Eligibility

A zombie may deliberately attack fleshmass through V4 only when every condition
is true:

- it is alive, spawned, able to act, and on the target's map;
- it belongs to the normal Zombieland zombie faction;
- its existing `raging` state is currently active;
- the global `ragingZombies` setting is enabled;
- `smashMode` is not `Nothing`;
- it is in its ordinary stumble/rage control flow;
- it has no higher-priority creature attack;
- it cannot continue a valid existing rage move;
- it is cardinally adjacent to the target; and
- it is a zombie that would perform an ordinary static melee attack rather than
  a bespoke special action.

V4 adds no special attacker multipliers.

### Special Zombies

- **Tanky zombies:** receive no V4-specific flesh rule. They already use special
  path and smash behavior and do not enter normal group rage.
- **Albino and dark-slimer zombies:** receive no V4-specific flesh rule because
  the existing rage trigger excludes them.
- **Suicide bombers:** do not receive a special gnaw action. If existing behavior
  arms or detonates one and the explosion damages flesh, that is incidental
  damage, not a V4 target decision.
- **Miners:** do not mine fleshmass. If otherwise eligible for ordinary rage and
  static melee, they use the same unmodified attack as a normal zombie.
- **Electrifiers, healers, toxic splashers, burning zombies, former-pawn
  zombies, and child zombies:** use existing hard-state and action priorities.
  If they reach the ordinary rage-obstruction attack path, V4 adds no speed,
  damage, or yield change.
- **Zombie spitters and symbiants:** are outside the ordinary `JobDriver_Stumble`
  rule and receive no new targeting behavior.

Incidental damage from any Zombieland-faction pawn or projectile may still
destroy flesh. If vanilla attributes the kill to that Zombieland-faction
instigator, the destroyed cell counts toward the response counter even when the
damage did not come from a deliberate V4 attack.

## Target Eligibility

An eligible deliberate target satisfies every condition below at selection and
again immediately before each new attack job starts:

- it is spawned `Fleshmass_Active`;
- it is on the zombie's map;
- it has a non-null `CompFleshmass.source`;
- that source is a spawned `Building_FleshmassHeart` on the same map;
- the cell is cardinally adjacent to the zombie;
- the zombie is genuinely blocked rather than merely standing beside the cell;
- the cell passes the protected-core rule for every spawned heart on the map;
- the cell is still damageable through ordinary vanilla building damage.

No target reservation is added. Several zombies may focus the same cell, just
as they can focus another ordinary static obstruction.

V4 does not require source-correct contiguous traversal for the vanilla
cascade. Exact source is required to establish that the root belongs to a live
heart encounter. After the root dies, the vanilla cascade remains authoritative
and may cross an unusual touching-field boundary exactly as vanilla does.

### Choosing Among Several Cells

If several eligible active cells are cardinally adjacent:

1. Prefer a cell obstructing the zombie's intended rage direction, when that
   direction is available.
2. Otherwise use the existing randomized cardinal scan order.
3. Do not prefer the heart, nerve bundles, low hit points, source identity,
   special mass, or a player-selected direction.

This keeps the attack as obstruction clearing rather than tactical fleshmass
mining.

## Protected Core And Final-Encounter Safety

The heart itself is never a target. Nerve bundles, spitters, flesh sacks, and
other special mass do not become V4 targets.

Protecting only the immediate touch ring is insufficient when vanilla may kill
eight additional cells in a cardinal cascade. V4 therefore uses a conservative
root-target buffer sized against the maximum installed cascade.

For every spawned `FleshmassHeart` on the map:

- calculate the minimum cardinal, Manhattan distance from the candidate target
  cell to that heart's 3-by-3 occupied rectangle;
- require that distance to be **at least 10 cells**; and
- reject the candidate if any heart fails that distance.

Why 10:

- a touch-ring cell is one cardinal step from the heart footprint;
- vanilla can remove at most eight additional cascade cells beyond the root;
- a root at distance 9 could, in the worst straight connected chain, reach a
  ring cell in eight additional kills; and
- a root at distance 10 cannot reach the ring within that cascade budget.

This is intentionally conservative and spatial rather than source-based because
vanilla cascade traversal does not enforce source ownership. It also protects a
nearby second heart when two fields touch.

The value 10 is not a balance tuning knob. It is a safety value derived from the
installed maximum cascade count and the heart interaction geometry. If either
vanilla value changes, re-derive it rather than exposing it as a setting.

V4 does not attempt to stop unrelated vanilla player damage, fire, explosions,
or third-party effects from reaching the core. It guarantees only that the new
deliberate zombie target rule cannot create the final approach.

## Attack And Destruction Semantics

### Normal Damage

V4 starts the existing single-attack `AttackStatic` job. It does not:

- call `Kill()` directly;
- set hit points to zero;
- add bonus damage;
- use mining damage;
- use a special flesh damage definition;
- skip melee cooldown;
- suppress armor, body type, consciousness, or manipulation effects;
- guarantee that an attack hits; or
- guarantee that one rage episode destroys a cell.

Multiple eligible raging zombies may attack the same cell. Ordinary main-thread
damage sequencing decides which attack kills it.

### Repeated Attacks

After one static attack job completes, the zombie returns to normal decision
flow. It may attack the same cell again only if:

- it remains raging;
- movement remains blocked;
- the cell remains eligible;
- no higher-priority target or action appears; and
- the ordinary melee cooldown and job flow permit another attack.

Once the collapse creates a valid route, movement wins and the zombie proceeds.
The zombie does not continue chewing neighboring flesh merely because it has
started.

### Vanilla Cascade

The killing hit uses ordinary damage information with the actual zombie as
instigator. V4 does not set `PreventCascade` on the root and does not replace
`CompCascadeOnDestroyed`.

The exact random cascade size, breadth-first cardinal order, early exhaustion,
effects, filth, and connected-cell selection remain vanilla behavior.

### Response Progress

A narrow postfix or equivalent hook on sourced fleshmass kill notification adds
the missing classification:

- if the kill has a Zombieland-faction instigator;
- and vanilla did not already count it through its null/player-faction rule;
- and the source grower is still spawned;
- call `Notify_FleshmassDestroyedByPlayer` exactly once for that destroyed cell.

Do not:

- call the notification twice for a factionless or player-faction instigator;
- add progress when damage does not kill the cell;
- add progress for cells merely enumerated by a predicted cascade;
- add progress when a kill is cancelled;
- add a custom counter;
- copy the 125–200 threshold into Zombieland;
- spawn defenders directly; or
- suppress the vanilla letter and effects.

The method name contains `ByPlayer`, but V4 intentionally reuses it as the
installed vanilla "destruction pressures the organism" counter. The patch
changes qualifying instigators, not the counter's mechanics.

## Relationship To Vanilla Growth

V4 adds no growth point when flesh dies.

After damage:

- vanilla marks the source as having lost mass;
- vanilla recalculates contiguous growth state when its own flow requires it;
- remaining vanilla growth points continue to be spent at the vanilla cadence;
- normal growth cycles and mini-growth continue;
- special organs and independent fleshbeasts remain possible; and
- detached or unusual shapes receive whatever treatment the installed vanilla
  implementation normally gives them.

This means a zombie collapse can cause a net loss. That is acceptable because:

- deliberate attacks are rage- and obstruction-gated;
- the protected core cannot be deliberately targeted;
- ordinary damage may require repeated attacks;
- the horde itself remains dangerous;
- every destroyed cell advances the defender-response counter; and
- the heart retains its own substantial growth system.

Do not restore strict one-for-one accounting unless live testing proves that
vanilla growth and response fail to provide enough counterpressure. Such a
finding would reopen the design, not justify quietly reintroducing V3's queue.

## Multiple Hearts And Touching Fields

The deliberate root target must have an exact spawned heart source. The
protected distance is checked against every spawned heart, not only that source.

Once the root dies, vanilla cascade behavior is authoritative. Because vanilla
cascade follows adjacent eligible defs rather than source ownership, an unusual
touching-field layout may let one collapse remove cells belonging to two hearts.

V4 accepts that as vanilla biological chaos. Response progress is applied per
destroyed cell to that cell's own source, so each affected heart receives only
the notifications produced by its cells.

Required outcomes:

- no deliberate root is inside either heart's protected core;
- no custom relocation or credit crosses between hearts;
- a vanilla cross-field cascade is not reimplemented or reassigned;
- each destroyed cell advances at most one source counter; and
- a missing, dead, despawned, or wrong-map source receives no custom progress.

## Interruption, Concurrency, And Save/Load

V4 stores no new long-lived feature state.

### Interruption

The existing `AttackStatic` job owns interruption. If the zombie dies, despawns,
is downed, changes map, receives a higher-priority job, or loses its target, the
ordinary job ends. No relocation debt or reservation remains.

### Concurrent Attackers

Several zombies may select the same cell. This is normal melee focus fire. Once
the cell dies, later attack jobs lose their target and return to normal flow.
Only the actual kill and actual vanilla cascade produce response progress.

### Save During An Attack

Use the existing job's save/load behavior. There is no new pending chew,
reservation, origin, destination, queue, or pulse to serialize. After load:

- a valid ordinary attack may resume through vanilla job handling; or
- an invalid job may end and be reconsidered normally.

No map change occurs until ordinary damage is applied, and each actual killed
cell is processed once through the kill notification path.

### Heart Removed Mid-Attack

If the heart or source disappears before a new attack begins, target
revalidation fails. If an already-issued ordinary hit lands while the target
still exists, vanilla damage resolves normally. Custom response progress is
added only if the source is still spawned, matching vanilla's own gate.

## Player Communication And Presentation

V4 uses existing presentation:

- rage eyes, rage thought bubble, and rage sound explain why a zombie became
  destructive;
- ordinary attack animation and impact audio explain the local damage;
- vanilla fleshmass destruction effects show the collapse;
- vanilla heart growth shows later resurgence; and
- vanilla fleshbeast birth effects and threat letter explain the accumulated
  response.

Do not add:

- a displacement meter;
- queue UI;
- heart pressure gizmos;
- per-cell wound graphics;
- a letter for every zombie attack;
- a message for every cascade; or
- a new tutorial popup.

At release, update the existing rage/smash help text and release notes with one
short behavior statement. Suggested meaning, not final localized wording:

> Raging zombie hordes blocked by living fleshmass may tear through its outer
> growth, provoking the organism's normal response.

## Performance Boundaries

V4 must remain local and event-driven.

- Do not index all fleshmass cells.
- Do not scan the map for fleshmass during normal pawn attack checks.
- Do not add a per-heart tick component.
- Do not add a per-zombie recurring random timer.
- Evaluate V4 only when an already-raging zombie reaches the existing blocked
  rage/smash decision.
- Inspect only cardinally adjacent cells for a root target.
- Heart-buffer checks may enumerate spawned hearts; normal maps contain very few
  and the check occurs only at a blocked rage boundary.
- Let vanilla own cascade traversal, damage, growth, and defender spawning.

The only map-wide lookup that may need a small cache is the current spawned-heart
list. Do not introduce a cache unless profiling shows the direct lookup matters.

## Tuning Policy

V4 deliberately introduces no new numeric balance setting.

The existing controls already provide natural tuning:

- `ragingZombies` enables or disables deliberate interaction;
- `zombieRageLevel` changes the horde density required for rage and its duration;
- `smashMode == Nothing` opts out of environmental destruction;
- zombie melee damage and the existing global damage factor affect time to kill;
- ordinary melee cooldown limits repeated attacks;
- the number of blocked front-line zombies limits simultaneous damage;
- vanilla random cascade size varies the opening;
- vanilla 125–200 response threshold varies the counterattack timing; and
- vanilla threat points vary the resulting fleshbeasts.

Do not add a "chance to attack fleshmass" slider before live testing. Rage
occurrence, path obstruction, adjacency, ordinary damage, and cascade randomness
already provide several independent sources of variation.

If testing proves the interaction too frequent, adjust in this order:

1. Require a stronger definition of genuinely blocked rage movement.
2. Require the candidate to align more closely with the rage direction.
3. Require a short existing-style hesitation before starting the first attack.
4. Only then consider an internal mean-time gate, still with no player setting.

If testing proves it too rare, adjust in this order:

1. Confirm rage movement actually reaches the blocked-smash branch beside
   fleshmass.
2. Allow a slightly wider forward arc while remaining local.
3. Let two adjacent blocked front cells qualify instead of only the exact
   forward cell.
4. Only then consider a local attraction rule.

Do not solve frequency problems by indexing the field or attracting the whole
map.

## Edge-Case Contract

| Situation | Required behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Calm zombie beside active fleshmass | Ignores it. |
| Any zombie beside inactive `Fleshmass` | Receives no V4 target; only `Fleshmass_Active` qualifies. |
| Raging zombie with a valid movement step | Moves; does not attack flesh. |
| Raging zombie blocked beside safe outer active flesh, with no higher-priority action | Starts one ordinary static attack; no extra probability roll is made. |
| Raging zombie beside flesh but with an adjacent valid pawn target | Attacks the pawn first. |
| `ragingZombies` disabled | No deliberate V4 attack. |
| `smashMode == Nothing` | No deliberate V4 attack. |
| `smashMode == DoorsOnly` | Safe blocking flesh is the one biological exception for actually raging zombies. |
| `smashMode == AnyBuilding` | Fleshmass remains rage-only; calm generic smash scanning must not target it. |
| `OnlyColonists`, `OnlyHumans`, or `Everything` | Same V4 obstruction behavior; attack mode still governs creatures only. |
| Cell merely damaged | No cascade and no response progress. |
| Root cell killed by eligible zombie | Vanilla cascade runs and every actually destroyed sourced cell advances response progress once. |
| Cascade has fewer than four neighbors | It ends naturally after available eligible cells are exhausted. |
| Candidate is less than 10 cardinal cells from any heart footprint | Not a deliberate V4 target. |
| Cascade crosses into another heart's touching field | Accept vanilla removal; credit each destroyed cell to its own source once. |
| Heart or source missing/despawned | No deliberate target; no custom response progress. |
| Several zombies attack one cell | Ordinary combined damage; one actual kill and one vanilla cascade. |
| Rage ends between attacks | Zombie stops receiving the V4 exception and returns to normal behavior. |
| Collapse opens movement | Movement wins; zombies do not mine the new perimeter automatically. |
| Collapse fails to open movement | A still-raging, still-blocked zombie may later attack an eligible cell again. |
| Player exploits a zombie-made opening | Allowed. The protected core, horde, and response system remain the counterweight. |
| Player blocks all outer flesh behind walls | V4 may never occur; it does not gain special pathing or wall knowledge. |
| Fire or explosion kills flesh | Vanilla behavior; count response progress when the actual instigator belongs to the Zombieland faction and vanilla did not already count it. |
| Save/load during attack | Existing job behavior only; no custom state duplication. |
| Heart analyzed | Same core protection; zombies cannot create the final interaction route. |
| Heart not analyzed | Same behavior; no special exposure reward. |
| Fleshbeasts emerge | Existing Anomaly/Zombieland hostility settings remain authoritative. |
| No nearby standable birth cell for a response | Vanilla response behavior decides; V4 does not substitute a spawn point. |
| Third-party mod changes cascade count | Static audit must fail until the protected distance is re-derived. |

## Implementation Surface

The expected code change is deliberately narrow:

1. Add a helper that identifies a cardinally adjacent safe active-fleshmass
   obstruction for an already-raging, movement-blocked zombie.
2. Call that helper from the existing rage/smash fallback and start the existing
   one-attack `AttackStatic` job.
3. Ensure generic building-smash scanning does not separately make active
   fleshmass a calm-zombie target under `AnyBuilding`.
4. Add a narrow Harmony postfix or equivalent hook that forwards qualifying
   Zombieland-faction fleshmass kills to the existing vanilla destruction-response
   notification exactly once.
5. Add no new serialized gameplay type unless implementation evidence proves an
   existing job boundary cannot express the behavior.

Likely reusable local concepts:

- `JobDriver_Stumble.TickAction` priority order;
- `ZombieStateHandler.RageMove` blocked-movement fallback;
- `ZombieStateHandler.Smash` and `AttackThing`;
- the randomized cardinal scan order;
- `ZombieBootstrap`'s faction invariant; and
- existing Anomaly hostility overrides for spawned fleshbeasts.

Before coding, re-decompile and record the exact members in the patch audit. Do
not implement from the names in this design document alone.

6. **Response accounting.** Prove every actually destroyed sourced root/child

### Combined Player Scenario

Use one reusable scenario fixture containing:

- an analyzed 3×3 heart and protected core;
- a long outer tendril crossing the rage route toward the colony;
- one distance-9 rejection cell and one distance-10 eligible cell;
- a dense horde capable of natural rage;
- a valid alternate route that can be opened during the test;
- enough preloaded vanilla response progress to cause one birth after a known
  number of destroyed cells;
- two touching outer fields beyond both hearts' protected cores; and
- save/reload immediately before the killing hit.

The scenario should prove the player-facing sequence: calm milling, natural rage,
blocked attack, ordinary damage, sudden vanilla collapse, horde movement through
the breach, accumulated fleshbeast response, existing hostility behavior,
vanilla heart regrowth, and warning-or-higher log cleanliness.

## Acceptance Criteria

V4 is ready to implement beyond a prototype only when all of these are true:

- calm zombies never deliberately attack fleshmass;
- no zombie is attracted to fleshmass from elsewhere on the map;
- only an already-raging, movement-blocked, ordinary static attacker can receive
  the deliberate obstruction exception;
- `smashMode == Nothing` is an absolute deliberate-attack opt-out;
- `attackMode` remains a creature-target policy and never indexes fleshmass;
- ordinary damage, cooldown, hit chance, and job interruption remain intact;
- nonlethal damage causes neither cascade nor response progress;
- the killing hit uses the actual zombie instigator and the full vanilla
  cascade;
- every actually destroyed sourced cell advances the vanilla response counter
  exactly once for a Zombieland-faction instigator;
- V4 never predicts cascade count, creates its own response counter, or spawns
  fleshbeasts directly;
- no deliberate root target lies closer than distance 10 to any heart footprint;
- a maximum installed cascade cannot remove the final touch ring;
- generic `AnyBuilding` scanning cannot turn flesh into a calm-zombie target;
- movement immediately retakes priority when a route opens;
- touching fields retain vanilla cascade behavior and per-cell source
  accounting;
- save/load and concurrent attackers create no duplicate hit, cascade, or
  response credit;
- normal heart growth and defender systems remain unmodified;
- existing Anomaly hostility settings control all resulting pawn relationships;
  and
- the combined scenario is warning-and-error clean.

## Explicitly Deferred Or Rejected

Do not add these during the first implementation:

- direct heart damage;
- heart interaction by zombies;
- one-for-one relocation;
- replacement queues or reservations;
- zombie-generated growth points;
- wound memory or directional growth history;
- defender budgets separate from vanilla response;
- special-zombie flesh damage multipliers;
- a fixed percentage of flesh-curious zombies;
- a per-zombie flesh-interest cooldown;
- a new flesh-attack chance slider;
- heart scent, pheromone emission, or global attraction;
- a map-wide fleshmass index;
- custom flesh damage, jobs, animations, sounds, motes, or letters;
- source-filtered reimplementation of vanilla cascade; or
- automatic cleanup of detached mass beyond vanilla behavior.

Reconsider one only after the V4 combined scenario demonstrates a specific
player-facing failure that the existing systems cannot solve.

## Final Design Summary

V4 is an interaction between two existing systems, not a new system between
them:

1. A dense zombie group naturally becomes enraged.
2. A front-line raging zombie is blocked by distant outer active fleshmass.
3. The zombie uses its ordinary static melee attack.
4. Ordinary damage eventually kills one cell.
5. Vanilla collapses up to four through eight additional cells.
6. The opening may help the player, the horde, or neither.
7. Every destroyed sourced cell pressures the existing vanilla response meter.
8. Vanilla eventually births fleshbeasts in a threat-scaled assault.
9. Existing hostility settings determine the resulting fight.
10. Vanilla heart growth later changes the field again.

The mechanic is rare because rage, obstruction, adjacency, ordinary damage, and
survival must all align. It scales with a horde because only blocked front-line
zombies can contribute damage. It cannot solve the heart because the maximum
vanilla cascade cannot reach the protected core. It remains unpredictable
because collapse size, field shape, horde position, response timing, defender
composition, hostility settings, and later heart growth all come from existing
systems.

That is the intended Zombieland outcome: the horde tears at the living terrain,
the terrain gives way unpredictably, and the organism answers in its own time.
